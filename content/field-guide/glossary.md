<dl class="terms">
<dt>Ambient interface</dt>
<dd>Information shown in the environment rather than on a device: a label floating beside a plant, a capacity reading above a walkway. The aesthetic's preferred way to show that technology is present.</dd>
<dt>Atoll</dt>
<dd>A ring-shaped island enclosing a lagoon. Used in the gallery for any ring-shaped enclave at sea.</dd>
<dt>Breakaway civilization</dt>
<dd>A small group living with technology a step ahead of the wider world, in a place of its own choosing. The default inhabitants of a Cyberparadist enclave.</dd>
<dt>Closed loop</dt>
<dd>A system in which energy, water, waste and food circulate with little loss. Rarely drawn, usually implied by the absence of smoke, pipes and refuse.</dd>
<dt>Cyber Paradise, Cybergenesis, Neogenesis</dt>
<dd>Alternative names for the aesthetic, used in different communities. This site uses Cyberparadism throughout.</dd>
<dt>Cyberprep</dt>
<dd>A neighbouring aesthetic of affluent, frictionless, clean futures. Shares Cyberparadism's materials; lacks its wilderness.</dd>
<dt>Enclave</dt>
<dd>The bounded human habitat at the center of most Cyberparadist images: a campus, garden, dome, crater city or terrace set inside a much larger natural world.</dd>
<dt>Enclosure</dt>
<dd>The compositional pattern in which the enclave's boundary is visible: a ring, a shell, a lattice, a shoreline.</dd>
<dt>Ephemeralization</dt>
<dd>Buckminster Fuller's term for doing more and more with less and less. The idea behind "minimalism by sophistication".</dd>
<dt>Field guide</dt>
<dd>This reference. A working description of the aesthetic, maintained in the open.</dd>
<dt>Folly</dt>
<dd>The vivid red-pink accent in the palette, used at most once per scene.</dd>
<dt>Junglecore</dt>
<dd>An aesthetic of dense, saturated tropical nature. Supplies much of Cyberparadism's environmental staging.</dd>
<dt>Lattice</dt>
<dd>A repeating structural grid, usually hexagonal or triangular, used for shells, glazing and bridges. The most common way to make a large enclosure look light.</dd>
<dt>Minimalism by sophistication</dt>
<dd>The principle that mature technology looks simpler, not more complicated: fewer visible machines, fewer seams, more integration.</dd>
<dt>Motif</dt>
<dd>A recurring visual element. In the gallery, pieces are filed by motif (dome, lattice, ring, waterfall) as well as by setting.</dd>
<dt>Post-cyberpunk</dt>
<dd>Work that keeps cyberpunk's advanced technology but drops its social decay. Cyberparadism is one of its moods.</dd>
<dt>Provenance</dt>
<dd>How a piece was made and by whom. Stated on every gallery page: creator, method, tool, date.</dd>
<dt>Setting</dt>
<dd>Where a piece takes place. The gallery uses five: coastal, highland, interior, sky and exoplanet.</dd>
<dt>Signs</dt>
<dd>In the manifesto, the technologies that point toward a cyberparadise: fusion energy, anti-gravity, synthetic life engineering, terraforming, full automation and open-source collaboration.</dd>
<dt>Solarpunk</dt>
<dd>A neighbouring aesthetic of green, communal, hand-maintained futures with an explicit politics. See the distinctions chapter.</dd>
<dt>Spaceship Earth</dt>
<dd>The planet understood as one vessel with finite resources and a shared crew. The enclave written large.</dd>
<dt>Terraforming</dt>
<dd>Deliberately modifying a planetary environment to make it habitable. One of the aesthetic's settings and one of the manifesto's Signs.</dd>
</dl>
