A frequently cited Cyberparadism palette emphasizes saturated aquas and greens with high-contrast neutrals and a single vivid accent. Creators treat it as a guideline rather than a constraint: aquas and greens communicate water, canopy and coastal brightness; silvers and whites communicate precision and cleanliness; the accent introduces signal, such as an alert, a highlight or a brand mark.

## Proportions

A scene that follows the palette tends to divide roughly like this:

| Share | Colors | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Most of the frame | Jungle green, teal, turquoise, white | Canopy, water, sky, haze |
| The enclave | Platinum, silver, white | Ceramic, glass, pale stone |
| Shadow and depth | Charcoal, teal | Shade under canopy, deep water |
| Signal | Aquamarine, cyan | Interface light, glow from within glass |
| One accent | Folly | A flower, a flag, a warning light, never two |

Folly is the easiest color to overuse. One occurrence reads as deliberate; several read as a theme park.

## Materials

Colors arrive attached to materials. Platinum is ceramic and bright concrete. Silver is machined metal, used thinly: rails, seams, the edge of a platform. White is cloud, glare and blank composite. Teal and turquoise are water seen at two depths. Jungle green is foliage, rarely paint. Charcoal is shadow first and material second.

## On this site

The interface you are reading uses the same palette. The day theme sets charcoal ink on a platinum ground with teal as the link color. The night theme sets platinum ink on a deep charcoal ground with aquamarine as the link color. Jungle green marks the active navigation item, and folly is held in reserve for warnings. Every text and background pair is checked for a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1, which is why the teal used for links on light surfaces is a shade darker than the swatch above; pure teal on platinum sits just below the threshold.
