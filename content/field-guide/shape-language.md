Cyberparadism favors **simple geometric primitives** and **biomimetic geometry**, often repeated across structures. The shapes are read as both aesthetic, because they are clean and legible, and functional, because they are efficient, modular and sustainable. Below is the working vocabulary.

{{ <shapes /> }}

## How the shapes combine

- **One dominant form per structure.** A dome is a dome; a tower is a cone or a helix. Buildings that are three shapes at once read as cluttered.
- **Repetition across the enclave.** The same primitive at several scales ties a settlement together: a large dome, smaller domes, dome-shaped pavilions.
- **Lattice as skin.** Hexagonal or triangular lattices appear on shells, glazing and bridges. They let a large enclosure look light.
- **Biomimicry where the structure grows.** Branching columns, helical ramps and fractal canopies suggest that the building was cultivated rather than assembled.
- **Rings hold space open.** Atolls, toruses and ring stations are enclosure without a roof: the center stays a lagoon, a garden or a view.

## What the shapes are not

Not brutalist mass, not rectilinear office slabs, not the exposed trusses and pipework of industrial science fiction. Right angles exist but recede; the eye should land on curves, facets and lattices. Where a rectangle is unavoidable, it is low, long and set into terrain.

## Using the vocabulary

For makers, the shape list doubles as a checklist. A scene with a dome, a lattice and a ring is already most of the way to reading as Cyberparadist, provided the [landscape](@/field-guide/visual-language.md#landscape-and-environment) around it is big enough and wild enough. The [emblem](@/about.md#the-emblem) of this site is a hexagon for the same reason.
