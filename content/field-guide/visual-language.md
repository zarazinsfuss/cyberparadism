If the [ethos](@/field-guide/overview.md#core-ethos) is the why, this chapter is the what. Four layers build most Cyberparadist images: the landscape, the built enclave, the technology cues, and the way they are composed.

## Landscape and environment

Cyberparadism foregrounds **wide natural scenes**:

- dense, saturated greens, with a clear adjacency to junglecore;
- coastal and tropical environments: beaches, lagoons, bright horizons;
- dramatic geology: cliffs, volcanic ridges, desert expanses, waterfalls;
- barren exoplanet surfaces contrasted with carefully maintained habitats.

The environment usually dominates the composition. The human habitat reads as a **deliberate insertion** into the world rather than a replacement of it. A good rule of thumb: if the built world takes more than a third of the frame, the image is drifting toward a different aesthetic.

## Architecture and the enclave

The built environment commonly appears as:

- **small, coherent clusters**, not planet-wide megacities;
- **highly refined minimalism**: clean surfaces, few visible seams;
- **materials that read as durable and future-clean**: white ceramics, polished stone, bright concrete, glass-like composites;
- **public spaces designed for gathering**: plazas, amphitheater forms, promenades, pools.

A recurring compositional pattern is **enclosure**. The habitat is framed as a bounded garden, campus, dome, crater-city or terraced complex within a much larger wilderness. The boundary is usually visible: a ring, a shell, a lattice, a shoreline.

{{ <fig piece="the-halo-atoll" caption="Enclosure made literal: a ring of docks holds the lagoon, the domes and the spire." /> }}

## Interfaces and technology cues

Cyberparadism implies advanced technology without fetishizing machinery:

- **mind-based or sensory interfaces**: subtle wearables, ambient UI, minimal screens;
- automation that is **quiet and embedded**;
- biotech and nanotech suggested through surface textures or seamless integration rather than through visible apparatus.

When an interface is shown, it tends to float in the environment as a thin annotation rather than sit on a device: a label on a plant, a capacity reading on a walkway, a route drawn in the air. The information belongs to the place.

## Composition

Patterns that recur across the gallery and the wider body of work:

- **Scale ratio.** Nature large, enclave small, people smaller still. A single figure is common and establishes scale without crowding.
- **Horizon and depth.** A high horizon or an elevated viewpoint, so that the landscape can keep going behind the habitat. Aerial views are frequent.
- **Light.** Daylight, often late morning or early evening; soft haze over distance; glass that glows faintly from within. Night scenes exist but are rare, and when they appear they are starlit rather than neon-lit.
- **Order against wildness.** Clean geometry set against irregular foliage and rock. Neither side is tidied to match the other.
- **One vivid accent.** A flower bed, a flag of orange foliage, a single warning light. The rest of the palette stays in aquas, greens and neutrals; see the [palette](@/field-guide/palette.md).

## What to leave out

Smoke, rust, wires, crowds, advertising, visible weapons, clutter of any kind. Their absence is most of what makes the technology read as mature.
