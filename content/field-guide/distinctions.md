Aesthetics are easiest to define at their edges. These are the four neighbours Cyberparadism is most often confused with, and the test that separates them.

## Not cyberpunk

Cyberparadism typically avoids the "high tech, low life" framing and the city-as-cage visual grammar. Cyberpunk's technology is intrusive and its cities are total; Cyberparadism's technology is quiet and its settlements are small. Both are post-industrial, but one is a warning and the other is an invitation. The shared ancestor is visible in the name.

## Not solarpunk

Both depict green futures. Cyberparadism tends to be more high-tech, minimalist and engineered, while solarpunk is frequently associated with visible craft, DIY culture, communal artisanry and retrofitted cities. A solarpunk street has bicycles, market stalls and vines on brick; a Cyberparadist shore has a ceramic dome and nobody visibly working. Solarpunk also carries an explicit politics that Cyberparadism, as an aesthetic, deliberately does not.

## Not generic futuristic environmentalism

Green imagery alone does not make a work Cyberparadist. The aesthetic is defined by the *relationship* between nature and an advanced enclave: a bounded, mature, minimal habitat inside a dominant landscape. A forest with solar panels is environmentalism; a forest with a single lattice dome and a quiet dock is Cyberparadism.

## Not automatically utopia

Many works use the paradise surface to complicate it later: the enclave's costs, its exclusions, or a hidden form of control. That narrative move can appear inside otherwise optimistic styling. The aesthetic describes how things look, not whether the society behind them is good. The [manifesto](@/manifesto.md) states an optimistic ethos; a work can share the look and question the ethos.

## At a glance

| | Cyberparadism | Cyberpunk | Solarpunk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scale of the built world | Small enclave | Total city | Retrofitted city or town |
| Technology | Mature, quiet, embedded | Intrusive, hacked, visible | Appropriate, visible, hand-maintained |
| Nature | The dominant stage | Absent or paved over | Woven through the city |
| Mood | Serene, luxurious, optimistic | Anxious, gritty, noir | Hopeful, communal, busy |
| Materials | Ceramic, glass, pale stone | Concrete, neon, steel, rain | Wood, brick, textiles, plants |
| Politics | Not a program; an ethos | Dystopian critique | Explicit, communitarian |
