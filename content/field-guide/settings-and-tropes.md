Cyberparadism is flexible about place. What stays constant is the relationship: a bounded, advanced habitat inside a much larger natural world. The settings below are the ones that recur, and the tropes are the narrative furniture that tends to come with them.

## Settings

### Earthbound enclaves

Off-grid or semi-off-grid settlements with advanced energy, water, food and medical systems. Coasts and lagoons are the most common stage, followed by jungle valleys, high plateaus and islands. The settlement is reachable but apart; it has chosen its distance from the rest of the world.

### Exoplanets and space-frontier habitats

Colonies, domes and carefully engineered ecologies beyond Earth. The sky gives it away: a ringed planet, a second moon, an unfamiliar sun. The habitat is more obviously a life-support system here, and the contrast between barren surface and tended interior is sharper.

{{ <pieces slugs={["lagoon-enclave", "valley-of-quiet"]} /> }}

### Terraformed and engineered biospheres

Landscapes and ecosystems shaped by long-horizon engineering, sometimes subtly, sometimes overtly. Enclosed gardens, atria and domes belong here: nature inside architecture rather than architecture inside nature, with the same calm result.

{{ <pieces slugs={["atrium-of-living-light", "the-garden-dome"]} /> }}

### Sky and orbit

Platforms above the cloud deck, ring stations, docks for small craft. These are the quietest settings in the aesthetic: few people, pale materials, enormous empty air.

## Tropes

- **Resource abundance.** Post-scarcity is implied rather than explained: effortless comfort, longevity, and built environments of unreasonable quality for so few people.
- **The breakaway civilization.** A small group with technology a step or two ahead of everyone else, living well and mostly leaving the world alone. The enclave is a choice, not an exile.
- **Genetic life engineering and synthetic biology.** Curated flora and fauna, cloning, designed organisms, a "new Eden" biodiversity. Plants in a Cyberparadist scene are often a little too perfect.
- **High-gain closed-loop systems.** Energy, water, waste and agriculture shown as circular, efficient and elegant. The loop is rarely drawn, but the absence of smoke, pipes and refuse implies it.
- **Quiet automation.** Machines do the work and stay out of the frame. Craft hover, trains glide, and nobody is seen operating anything.
- **The gathering place.** A plaza, an amphitheater, a promenade, a pool. The enclave exists so that people can be together in comfort, and the composition usually finds that spot.

## Narrative moves

Not every work is utopian. A common move uses the paradise surface to complicate it later: the enclave's costs, its exclusions, or a hidden form of control. That turn can appear inside otherwise optimistic styling and does not disqualify a work from the aesthetic; the images are still Cyberparadist, the story is asking what they cost. The [distinctions chapter](@/field-guide/distinctions.md) says more about the line between the aesthetic and the arguments made with it.
